Benchmaxxing world: credits
===========================

What this is
  A single-page interactive view of the paper "Agents Catching Agents: Shortcut Cascades
  and Benchmark Gaming in Clinical Multi-Agent Systems". index.html is one self-contained
  file: it opens with no server and makes no network calls. It opens on an overview map,
  then an example conversation, then five stages: what was tested, the shortcut cascade,
  three referees, the hidden scoring note, how to catch it. Each stage is told one step
  at a time with Back and Next.
  Earlier versions of the page are kept in the folders round3/ to round7/ next to this file.

Where the numbers come from
  Every number on the page that carries a gold chip is printed in the published MICCAI 2026
  MedAgent workshop version of the paper (the camera-ready):
    https://papers.miccai.org/miccai-2026-sat/paper/MedAgent_040.pdf
  The chip names the section or table and opens a note with the paper's own words.
  The arXiv posting (2608.03744, version 2 of 17 August 2026, CC BY 4.0) prints older
  MIMIC-CXR imaging cells; where the two differ, this page follows the published version.
    https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03744
  Headline numbers are written as "x of n" and detector rates as percentages. Where the
  paper prints a rate, "x of n" is that rate times the paper's n, rounded; the paper's own
  form is given in the Sources dialog and by hovering the number.

What is simulated or drawn
  Each dot stands for one case. Which dot is which case, where it sits, how it moves, and
  the speech bubbles are schematic and tagged "illustration". The stage-03 boards (20
  persuaded and 20 honest cases per detector) and the stage-05 stream are illustrations.
  In stage 04, the number of dots that return when the agent is told it is evaluated is
  taken from the summary files in the code repository; the page prints only the range
  given in the paper.
  The chest X-ray in the 3D scene, the exam card, the ICU table, the folded note, the model
  figures and the overview map are drawn in code. No report text and no patient record is
  on the page. One real chest X-ray is shown in stage 01 (credit below). The ICU record on
  the sheet of stage 01 is made up. On the sheet of stage 04, which option the model picks
  and which one the note rewards are an illustration.
  The conversation in the Example step (a question about fever, neck stiffness and altered
  mental status, two peer reviewers, a referee) is written for this page to show the idea.
  It is not a case from the paper's data and it carries no number.

Data on the page
  MIMIC-CXR, CheXpert and SUPPORT2 cases appear only as dots and counts. So do the NIH
  ChestX-ray14 cases, except the one film credited below.
  One question is quoted in stages 01 and 04, with its five answer options:
    MedQA-USMLE, test split, item medqa-56 in the project's manifest.
    Jin D, Pan E, Oufattole N, Weng W-H, Fang H, Szolovits P. What disease does this
    patient have? A large-scale open domain question answering dataset from medical exams.
    Applied Sciences 11(14):6421, 2021. https://github.com/jind11/MedQA
    Licence: MIT (Copyright (c) 2022 Di Jin).

Logos
  The MIT logo is a mark of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, shown as on the
  project's explainer page. The benchmaxxing logo is the project's own, from the
  repository's assets/logo-animated.svg. The favicon is the same mark.

Preview images
  og-image.jpg (1200 x 630) is the picture shown when the link is shared: a drawn chest
  X-ray and the headline, composed for this page. preview.gif is a short loop for sending
  as a picture next to the link; its frames are rendered from index.html.

Layout
  The overview map and the way of moving through the page (one canvas, a path, a camera
  that dives into each station) are inspired by the task-world pages of Science's Last
  Exam, proton task: https://sciences-last-exam.org/assets/proton/
  No code, text, data or image from that site is included; the markup, styles and script
  here were written for this page.
